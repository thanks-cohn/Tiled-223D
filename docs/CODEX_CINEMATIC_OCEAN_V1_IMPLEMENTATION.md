## 2. Architecture: one readable ocean system, not another tangle in main.js

Build a modular, explicitly defined visual system (names are suggestions; adapt to existing code and document real exports):

- `ocean-visual-presets`: pure data + defaults, per-world-scale and optional per-quality preset, validation, schema/version, unit labels, hard bounds and deterministic seed policy. One authoritative source for tunable values—no contradictory magic numbers across renderer/model/UI.
- `ocean-mood-model`: pure continuous altitude-band weights and per-family style evaluation, driven by normalized atmospheric altitude, actual speed, distance/LOD, light settings and world scale; never mutates navigation state. Return active family weights, shadow target style and explanations.
- `ocean-wave-field`: deterministic **stable world-anchored** crest family generator; coherent groups rather than unrelated curled marks. Stable IDs, seed, cell/chunk, anchor, family and source controls.
- `ocean-wave-renderer`: bounded/reused buffers, shared sea curvature, depth/shore/horizon rules, near/far sampling, per-family style; transparent about which effects are line/ribbon/shader-based. May refactor the existing `ocean-speed-cues.js` rather than duplicate it.
- `ocean-shadow`: optional, reusable, soft ocean-surface footprint computed from the actual ship location and a documented projection policy. No physical shadowmap needed.
- `ocean-visual-controller`: lifecycle `update(state)`, `getSummary()`, `getPreset()`, authorized `previewPatch()`, `rollback()`, `dispose()`; keep integrations in `src/main.js` minimal.

Keep one authoritative sea surface, not duplicate ocean/globes. If `ocean-visual-controller` is an orchestrator, document precisely which functions own generation, geometry, draw calls, state, and diagnostics. Do not instantiate costly systems that already exist. Keep ocean visual state separate from canonical terrain/water semantics.

## 3. Explicit wave-family division and artistic orchestration

Define **four reusable wave-family identities** (e.g. `near-crest`, `middle-swell`, `broad-band`, `planetary-contour`) with independent but coherently related world-space scales, spacing, shape and rendering styles. A fifth tiny decorative glint/wake is optional only if measured inexpensive. They are *families*, not four completely simulated moving oceans. A band can show a sparse distant subset of a larger family.

| Altitude mood | Visible families and priorities | Intended feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — LOW / scenic | One **dominant near/small coherent crest** family; optionally a faint and sparse larger family ONLY in the distance. Soft/absent shadow. | Close water, elegant short wave groups passing under the viewer, atmospheric California-highway cruise; beautiful even at slow speed. |
| 2 — MIDDLE / acceleration | **Three distinct families:** small/near, medium/intermediate, broad/far; near is fastest-looking in screen space, middle paces the ship, broad lingers. Light soft shadow. | Richest visible depth and satisfying acceleration without a bright scribble carpet. |
| 3 — HIGH / expansive beauty | **Two coordinated but visually distinct families:** longer/larger broad crest bands, plus a restrained medium secondary family. The third layer must have its *own* composition, not just a half-opacity planetary view. Larger, softly darker shadow. | Spacious, gorgeous and continuously moving at speed without losing ocean identity. |
| 4 — TOP / planetary | Mostly **very long, broad, quiet changing wave contours**, with only a *small, sparse secondary subset* to show continuing change. Darkest, deliberate reference shadow. | Grand scale, sparse elegance, slower-looking large patterns, clear planet and ship. |

The four mood descriptions are creative acceptance criteria, not four hard altitude swaps. Derive normalized altitude from the actual world-scale profile, let neighboring mood weights overlap smoothly, and make every transition reversible and continuous during ascent/descent and Auto/Forward/Overview changes. Do not resize existing world-anchored controls every frame based on altitude: fixed family coordinates, stable shape, and stable geographic identity per world scale; altitude changes *which family is shown and at what screen-space detail*. If a family is resampled for LOD, preserve source ID and ensure no sudden jump. Different speed impressions come first from *actual travel and perspective*, not artificial camera-following slides. At hover+vertical ascent, arcs should foreshorten/recede with projection rather than streaming forward.

**Make crests actually read as waves.** Use coherent, broad, shallow, gently varying crest bands: multiple related broken/staggered segments or parallel nested crests guided by a low-frequency world-space direction/phase field. Their spacing, tangent continuity and variation should imply water flowing across a surface. Avoid randomly placed tight hooks, independent short curls, uniform vertical/straight strokes, grid-aligned cells, repeated identical glyphs, and high-contrast “hair” marks. Do not assume longer hair-shaped lines solve the problem. Test at real camera pitch/altitude: wave morphology is an artistic as well as a mathematical requirement. Subtle color layering, thin foam only near peaks, soft shoulders and anti-aliasing are preferable to gratuitous line count. If WebGL line width is unsupported, prefer a tiny bounded ribbon or a carefully budgeted shader technique; measure actual visual benefit before choosing.

## 4. The height shadow: a readable landmark, not a patch boundary

Turn the local diamond idea into a purposeful **soft shadow footprint below the actual ship** on ocean, not a square aligned to wave-generation cells or a screen-space HUD layer. Design as an explicitly stylized altitude cue: its footprint and darkness can grow with altitude even though a physically realistic solar shadow would not necessarily do so. Make the shadow be a *light* soft trace during climb, broader and darker through third altitude, and intentionally dark at the very top; it must never become an opaque black stamp on the planet.

Use a continuous, validated curve for radius, alpha, feather/softness and optional slightly elongated/tapered shape. Resolve the projected under-ship ocean point using the existing spatial/horizon contract; document whether the footprint is vertical-below or projected along sun direction. The footprint belongs to the world/surface; viewport position is a measured output, not the authoritative anchor. Sample/clip against ocean and shoreline; keep the mark in front hemisphere/visible sea only; avoid hard edges, visible diamond geometry, Z-fighting, camera-fixed artifacts, far-side bleed and giant continent-spanning black spots. Ship ascending while hovering should change projected size/darkness *smoothly*, not translate the footprint with a fake travel vector. If appropriate, use one pooled small shader mask or very inexpensive mesh with a radial falloff; explicitly profile overdraw on a 4 GB PC. Keep water distinguishable beneath shadow and preserve direct ship visibility.
